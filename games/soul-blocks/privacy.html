Privacy Policy

Soul Blocks · PL Labs · Last updated September 14, 2026

The short version: Soul Blocks does not collect, send, sell or share any personal information. There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Your scores and settings are saved only on your own device.

This policy explains how Soul Blocks, a mobile game published by PL Labs (“we”, “us”), handles information. It applies to the Soul Blocks app for Android.

Information we collect

None. Soul Blocks does not ask for your name, email address, phone number, location, contacts or any other personal information. You don’t need an account to play, and the game has no sign-in.

The game does not send your gameplay, scores, device information or identifiers to us or to anyone else. We have no servers that receive data from the app, and we can’t see who plays or how.

Data stored on your device

To work properly, the game saves a small amount of information in the app’s private storage on your device. It never leaves your device through the game:

WhatWhy
Best scoresTo show your best score for each mode.
SettingsTo remember your choices for sound, haptics, screen shake and room style.
Run in progressSo you can leave the game and pick up the same run later.
Daily Rite resultsTo record whether you completed each day’s rite, and in how many pieces, so each day can be played once.

None of this identifies you. Uninstalling the game or clearing its data in your phone’s settings deletes all of it.

Android backup. If you have Android’s own backup turned on, your phone may include the game’s saved data in its backup to your Google account, so your progress can come back when you set up a new phone. That backup is run by Google, under Google’s Privacy Policy, and we never have access to it. You can turn it off in your device’s backup settings.

Advertising

Soul Blocks does not show ads and contains no advertising SDKs. It does not use your advertising ID.

Analytics and tracking

The game has no analytics, crash-reporting or tracking tools, and it doesn’t use cookies.

Purchases

Soul Blocks is free and has no in-app purchases.

Permissions

The app does not request access to your contacts, camera, microphone, photos, files or location.

  • Internet: the app’s Android package includes the standard internet permission that its app framework adds by default. The current version of the game does not use it to send or receive data, and plays fully offline.
  • Vibration: if haptics are turned on, the game uses your phone’s vibration for short taps when you place pieces and clear lines. You can turn this off in the game’s settings.

Google Play

If you download Soul Blocks from Google Play, Google handles your download and may collect information about it (for example, install counts and anonymous crash reports that Android shares with developers) under Google’s Privacy Policy. We only see this as totals and summaries in the Play Console, and it doesn’t identify you.

Children

Soul Blocks does not collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13. Because the game gathers no data at all, there is nothing about a child for us to hold. If you have a question about this, contact us using the email below.

Your rights and deleting your data

Because we don’t collect or store any personal data, we hold nothing about you to access, correct, export or delete. All of the game’s data lives on your device, and you are in control of it: to delete it, uninstall the game or go to Settings → Apps → Soul Blocks → Storage → Clear data on your phone (the exact menu names vary by device).

This website

This page is hosted by Netlify. Like most web hosts, Netlify may keep standard server logs (such as IP address and browser type) to run and protect the site. Our pages load fonts from Google Fonts, which means your browser contacts Google’s servers. We don’t use analytics or tracking cookies on this site.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of Soul Blocks changes how it handles information, for example by adding online features or ads, we will update this page before that version is released and change the date at the top. Features like that would always be described here first.
